The problem

Benefit and care-service forms are long, technical and stressful. Many people, particularly vulnerable adults applying for support such as Scotland's Adult Disability Payment, rely on a support worker or family member to complete them.

Working in Scottish health and social care, I saw this first-hand. CORES explores whether an AI assistant grounded in the actual forms can give people clear, patient, step-by-step guidance, so they can complete forms more independently.

How it works

CORES uses retrieval-augmented generation: answers are grounded in the forms themselves, not just the model's general knowledge.

  • Reading scanned forms. PyMuPDF and docx2txt extract text, and Tesseract OCR reads text inside images, because many real forms are scans.
  • Search. Text is split into overlapping chunks, embedded with OpenAI embeddings and stored in a FAISS vector index. Each question retrieves the three most relevant passages.
  • Answering. GPT-4, via LangChain, combines the retrieved passages, recent conversation and a detailed set of instructions.
  • Measuring. Every answer is timed, every session is logged, and users rate the experience at the end.

Designing the assistant's behaviour

Much of the work went into the instructions that shape how CORES behaves with vulnerable users. It is told to:

  • use short, friendly, culturally sensitive sentences, one per line;
  • guide one field at a time and check each before moving on;
  • point to the relevant form, section and page;
  • ask the user to rephrase rather than guess when it cannot find an answer;
  • stay on task when a conversation drifts, and offer reassurance throughout.

In testing, it corrected its own mistake when a user challenged it:

User: Where is this asked? I cannot find the form.

CORES: I apologise for the confusion. The section for your personal details is on the Referral Form, not the Nutritional Risk Assessment form. My mistake for the mix-up.

Results

Accuracy: 22 of 24 test exchanges were correct, giving 91.67% (correct responses ÷ total queries × 100).

Speed depends on the question. Questions needing detailed, grounded answers take longer; simple or off-topic messages get short, quick replies:

Question typeAverage response
Logical / clarification6.44 s
Procedural4.85 s
Inappropriate / incoherent2.39 s
Identity / existential2.20 s

Usability: early user testing was positive, especially about the step-by-step approach and the clarity of instructions, and most session ratings were high.

Responsible AI and data protection

  • No real personal data is shown on this page or in the published code.
  • The original forms are not published; they belong to their publisher.
  • API keys are never stored in the code.
  • The assistant is instructed to stay within its purpose and ask for clarification rather than invent answers.

Limitations and next steps

CORES is a research prototype, not an approved government or clinical service. The evaluation used a small number of test exchanges and users, so larger-scale testing is the essential next step. The assistant follows official mygov.scot procedures through its instructions rather than fetching the website live, and it runs as a text chat rather than a web app.

Next, I would add a web interface, live retrieval from official guidance with source links, and broader evaluation. The same approach can serve any organisation that needs to guide people through its own forms and policies.
